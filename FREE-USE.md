# RunMat Free Use

This file is the Additional Use Grant of the Business Source License in
LICENSE.md. It applies to the version of RunMat it is distributed with.
Defined terms are in section 7.

## 1. Who can use RunMat for free

You may make production use of RunMat without a commercial license if
you are:

- (a) an individual, working on your own behalf, including freelance work;
- (b) a student or faculty member at an academic institution, for teaching
  or research there;
- (c) a contributor to an open-source project, for work on that project; or
- (d) an organization with 250 or fewer people and US$10 million or less in
  annual revenue.

Anyone may make non-production use of RunMat.

## 2. Building on RunMat

You may build software that runs on RunMat and share it however you
like: toolboxes, packages, libraries, scripts, simulation studies and
compiled programs, free or paid, open source or not. You may include
RunMat with what you share so its users can run it.

Compiling your software with RunMat, or including RunMat with it, does
not extend the production-use rights granted by this license.

This grant does not cover a Competing Offering (see its definition in
section 7), such as a hosted RunMat service.

## 3. Simulation Usage

Production use of the FEA Engine under section 1 includes the free
simulation allowance stated at https://runmat.com/pricing for this
version of RunMat, or any larger allowance the Licensor provides.
That page specifies the allowance and its renewal period and forms
part of this grant for those purposes.

Simulation usage is calculated under the version of RunMat Simulation
Usage Accounting identified by the executing version of RunMat. One
GFLOP is one billion measured floating-point operations.

Loading, viewing or reusing previously computed outputs, including
meshes and simulation results, is unmetered. New meshing and solving
work is measured separately.

Eligible academic institutions may use the FEA Engine without
simulation usage charges after the Licensor verifies and approves
their eligibility. Request eligibility verification at
https://runmat.com/contact?type=academic.

A subscription or commercial agreement may provide an additional
simulation allowance.

## 4. Growing past the size limit

An organization that was eligible under section 1(d) and grows past its
limits may continue its production use for 90 days, once. After that, it
needs a commercial license.

## 5. Later versions

These terms stay fixed for the version of RunMat they ship with. If a
later version has different terms, they apply only to that later
version.

## 6. Extending these terms

A RunMat subscription or commercial license from the Licensor may extend
this grant as stated in its terms.

## 7. Definitions

- **"RunMat"** means the Licensed Work.

- **"Production use"** means any use of RunMat whose results are used or
  relied on, including in research and engineering. Any other use, such as
  trying, testing or developing with RunMat, is non-production use.

  under common control with an organization. "Control" means owning more
  than 50% of the voting interests of an entity, or otherwise having the
  power to direct its management and policies.

- **"Academic institution"** means an accredited college or university. It
  does not include a Federally Funded Research and Development Center

  by the U.S. government, even when a university operates it.

- **"Organization"** means a company, nonprofit organization, government

  whether or not it charges directly, whose users come to it to run their
  own code or simulation studies on RunMat, such as a hosted RunMat
  service or a RunMat-based editor, notebook or agent environment.
  Software that runs on RunMat, such as a toolbox or program, is not one,
  even when RunMat is included with it, and neither is use within an
  organization and its Affiliates.

- **"FEA Engine"** means the part of RunMat that meshes and solves finite
  element models, however it is invoked.

- **"Annual revenue"** means gross revenue in the most recently completed
  fiscal year, or total budget for an organization without revenue, such
  as a government body, converted to US dollars at the exchange rate on
  the last day of that year.
